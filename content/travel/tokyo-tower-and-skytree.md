It was October 2024, close to Halloween. I started the day at JR East Hotel Mets Akihabara and left the hotel early, since I wanted to fit both Tokyo Tower and Tokyo Skytree into the same day. Days like that can turn into a checklist if I'm not careful, but I was in a good mood and felt like walking.

## Getting to Tokyo Tower

From the hotel, I made my way to Akihabara Station and took the Hibiya Line to Kamiyacho Station. The moment I surfaced in Kamiyacho, it felt quieter than Akihabara. The side streets were narrow and there weren't many people around yet. It was a good change of pace.

Before I left the station, I spent a few minutes searching for an eki stamp. I eventually found it and stamped my notebook, which felt like a small win before the rest of the walking started.

From Kamiyacho, I continued on towards Tokyo Tower. I still think Tokyo has a unique way of making distances feel longer than they are. A ten minute walk can turn into thirty if you take a wrong turn or exit a station on the wrong side. I checked my phone more times than I wanted to admit, but I eventually made it.

## Tokyo Tower

Tokyo Tower is one of those places that looks familiar even if you've never seen it in person. The orange and white paint stands out immediately. It's a classic landmark and it still has a charm to it that doesn't feel forced.

When I arrived, I had to wait around a half hour outside before the tower opened. Workers were present prepping for the day and moving supplies around. A tour group waited nearby, clustered together in a loose line.

Inside, the process was straightforward. I bought a ticket, followed the signs, and went up to the main deck. The view from the main deck was exactly what I wanted: high enough to see how dense Tokyo is, but still close enough that the city looked real. I could pick out clusters of buildings, main roads, and patches of green.

After I spent time on the main deck, I went up to the top deck. The extra height made a difference. Tokyo became flatter and wider. The city looked endless in every direction, and it was easier to understand just how much of it is packed together. I took my time and walked a slow loop around the windows, stopping whenever I found an open spot.

Eventually, I headed back down and started the trip to Tokyo Skytree.

## Tokyo Skytree

Tokyo Skytree was impressive before I even entered the building. It's the kind of structure that makes you stop for a second and look up. The area around the base was busy, with families, couples, and plenty of other tourists who had the same plan.

In the courtyard outside, there was a children's event going on for Halloween. Kids were dressed in costumes and parents were taking photos. There were blow up pumpkins, music, and performers. It was the type of thing that felt normal in Tokyo, but would have turned heads back home.

I visited both the Tembo Deck and the Tembo Galleria. The Tembo Deck was a great view on its own, with the city laid out in every direction. Cars were reduced to small movement and trains looked like thin lines cutting between buildings.

Afterwards, I went up to the Tembo Galleria. That was the portion that really made me feel the height. I took another slow lap, looking out at the city and watching the crowd shift around the windows. Similar to Tokyo Tower, the best moments were the brief stretches where you could just stand still and look without being bumped or rushed.

## Heading Back to Akihabara

By the time I left Tokyo Skytree, I was tired in the specific way you get from being on your feet for hours while also being mentally alert. I had spent most of the day moving through stations, following signs, waiting in lines, and looking out at Tokyo from two different heights.

On the way back to Akihabara, I kept thinking about how different the two towers felt. Tokyo Tower has a classic personality to it. Tokyo Skytree feels modern and engineered for the view. I'm glad I did both in a single day. It felt like I saw two different versions of the same city.
